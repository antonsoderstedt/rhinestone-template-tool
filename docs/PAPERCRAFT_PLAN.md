# Papercraft studio: plan

**Status:** Phases 0 to 5 done (ADR 0037 to 0041, `docs/PAPERCRAFT_ENGINE_SPEC.md`, the engine, the studio at `/papercraft`, the PDF cover page, twenty built-in models, repair of imported models, and saving, sharing and syncing of models); simplifying of heavy models (ADR 0043); animals with legs (ADR 0044) and folds that close by themselves (ADR 0045); what is left is under "Later" below · **Date:** 2026-11-25

A third studio next to Rhinestone and HTV: make 3D paper models from low-poly meshes, then **print** the patterns or **cut and score**
them with a cutting machine, and fold and glue.

## Decisions (made with the owner, 2026-11-25)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| First version | **3D model to pattern** (unfold a mesh into parts with tabs, numbers and fold lines). Importing finished PDF patterns is later. |
| Output, first version | **PDF for printing** and **Cricut SVG** (separate cut and score layers). Silhouette DXF later. |
| Where the 3D models come from | **Import own files** (OBJ, STL, GLB) and **built-in generated shapes**. Shapes made from the tool's 2D shapes later. |
| Machine and paper | **Cricut Maker 3**, cardstock 160-250 g/m2 (also plain printing on 160 g/m2 paper). |

## What the reference patterns look like

The owner's templates are printed 2D patterns (Bison skull, Flamingo, Skull, Half skull; found in Desktop and Downloads, not attached):

- A cover page: assembled size in mm and inches (the flamingo is 607 x 163 x 427 mm), number of parts (49), paper weight, difficulty, time.
- Pattern sheets: solid cut outlines, **dashed fold lines** (mountain and valley are drawn differently), and **numbers on edges**: two edges with
  the same number are glued together. Large models are split over pages ("row1 col1").
- They contain **no 3D data**. They cannot be turned into a 3D model; they can only be re-used as flat patterns (a later feature).

**Licence:** at least one of them says the models may not be reproduced, changed, resold or shared. They are private reference files: not in
the repository, not on the public site, not built-in examples (same rule as the design collection, ADR 0030). Built-in examples are
generated by us.

## Where it lives

- New engine `src/lib/papercraft-engine/` (pure, deterministic, millimetres, no DOM, no network; no `Math.random()` or `Date.now()`),
  separate from the Rhinestone engine. The engine does not import from `/app/`.
- A new route `/papercraft`, a studio shell like HTV and Rhinestone, with the shared top navigation, language, units and sync (a new sync kind
  `papercraft` for saved models).
- `three` and `@react-three/fiber` (with `@react-three/drei`) are already dependencies (used for the HTV garment).
- Rules from AGENTS.md apply: engine first, tests before the UI, an ADR per decision, a spec document for the algorithm, no collision
  check switched off, SVG without nested `<svg>`, `<use>`, opacity below 1 on cut shapes, and never rasterised.

## Phases

### Phase 0: decisions and spec
ADR (papercraft engine and studio), `docs/PAPERCRAFT_ENGINE_SPEC.md` (the unfolding algorithm, tab rules, numbering, file formats), acceptance
criteria, and the export format for Cricut (layers and colours) to be confirmed with a physical test sheet.

### Phase 1: the engine (no screens)
1. **Mesh**: indexed mesh with vertex welding, face adjacency (half-edge), per-edge dihedral angle, validation (open edges, non-manifold edges,
   zero-area faces, flipped normals, tiny faces) with plain-language warnings. Planar quads and larger faces are kept as one face; other n-gons are triangulated.
2. **Readers**: OBJ and STL (ASCII and binary) in the engine; GLB is read in the app with three.js and handed over as vertices and faces.
3. **Scale**: model to a target size in mm (width, height or depth), with a report of the smallest edge and face.
4. **Unfolding**: choose cut edges (a spanning tree of the face graph, preferring long, flat, hidden edges), flatten faces into the plane
   one by one across shared edges, detect overlaps (polygon intersection, exact in mm with a tolerance), cut more edges until no part overlaps itself.
5. **Tabs and numbers**: a glue tab on one side of each cut edge pair (shape and width are settings; tab corners are clipped so a tab never lies
   over a face), matching numbers on the two edges, mountain or valley by dihedral angle and the chosen paper side.
6. **Pages**: pack parts on A4, Letter or a cutting mat (12 x 12 and 12 x 24 inch; usable area to be verified), with margins and rotation; parts
   bigger than a page are split along a fold with registration marks.
7. **Export**: SVG in mm with layers (cut, mountain fold, valley fold, numbers as outlined paths), a vector PDF writer (lines, dashes, outlined
   text) and the cover page.
8. **Tests**: cube, tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron, a generated low-poly animal and a non-convex shape. Checks include: after unfolding every
   face has the same area and edge lengths as in 3D; no overlap inside a part; every cut edge has one partner of equal length with the same
   number; tabs never overlap faces; all parts fit their page; same input gives the same output.

### Phase 2: the studio
A studio shell with three views: **3D** (the model, with an animated fold and unfold), **Pattern** (the parts on the page) and **Pages**
(the printed or cut sheets). Click an edge in the pattern to cut or join it and the model is unfolded again; move and rotate parts; select a face in 3D
and see it in the pattern. Properties: size, paper, tab width and shape, line styles, numbering, page size.

### Phase 3: printing and cutting
- **PDF** with a cover page like the references: assembled size, part count, paper, estimated time, difficulty, a page map, and instructions.
- **Cricut SVG**: cut and score layers in distinct colours (the Design Space steps for scoring are written next to the export, since Design
  Space assigns Cut/Score per layer after upload), numbers as a draw or print layer, sized to the mat.
- **A test sheet** (a small cube) to try paper weight, scoring and tab tolerance before a big model, like the Rhinestone test pack.

### Phase 4: models
Built-in generated shapes (cube, gem, icosahedron, vase, low-poly animals) and import with a check and fix (flip normals, fill holes where simple).

### Phase 5: polish
Saved models in My Designs and sync, Swedish, phone layout, share link, version history.

### Later
Import finished PDF patterns as cut files (read lines from the PDF), shapes from the tool's 2D shapes (extrude, lathe), coloured faces with print-then-cut,
Silhouette DXF, slot-together models (no glue), upright birds (penguin, owl) and animals with horns or a mane. (Simplifying a heavy model to low-poly is done: ADR 0043; four-legged animals: ADR 0044; birds on two legs: ADR 0046.)

## Risks and open points

- **Unfolding quality** is the hard part. Good tools differ in how few parts they make and how easy the model is to build. The first version will not match
  Pepakura, and the number of parts for a given model is not known until it is built.
- **Cutting** has not been tried: Design Space scoring, the usable mat area, and tab and fold tolerances need physical test sheets.
- **Numbers** as outlined paths (Design Space handles text poorly) on a draw or print layer; to be confirmed.
- **Mountain or valley** depends on which side of the paper faces out; one setting, checked on the cube.
- **Animals with legs** are blocky and rated expert (about 90 faces, 100 glued edges); whether the legs of a big one hold, and whether the small tab at a tail tip is workable, is not known until one is built.
- **Model quality** from the user's own files: open, non-manifold or very dense meshes get warnings; dense meshes are simplified when they are imported (ADR 0043).

## First milestone (shows something real)

After Phase 1 plus a thin studio: import or generate a cube and an icosahedron, see the net, print the PDF, export the SVG, cut the test cube
and build it.
